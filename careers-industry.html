Careers & industry

Drone careers are bigger than piloting.

Drone delivery & autonomous operations

A delivery network needs far more than a pilot.

Commercial drone delivery connects aviation, logistics, safety, airworthiness, data services, infrastructure, environmental planning, and community engagement. That makes it a useful example of how one drone operation can create many different career pathways.

01

Flight & Operations

Mission coordination, operations control, crew support, operating procedures, and exception management.

02

Safety & Compliance

Safety systems, regulatory coordination, documentation, quality assurance, and operational accountability.

03

Airworthiness & Reliability

Aircraft condition, maintenance governance, technical records, reliability, and continued operational safety.

04

Hub & Logistics Operations

Site readiness, loading workflows, fleet coordination, inventory movement, charging, and delivery infrastructure.

05

UTM, Data & Communications

Airspace-status data, traffic-management services, communications coverage, conformance monitoring, and system integration.

06

Community & Environmental Planning

Local coordination, public communication, land-use considerations, environmental review, noise, and community trust.

Industry signals

What is changing in the drone workforce.

Industry trends should be treated as a moving picture. These indicators are sourced from public FAA and U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics information and should be refreshed periodically.

FAA • Current operations

Part 135 package delivery is already operating.

Commercial UAS package delivery currently uses Part 135 air-carrier certification, while the final BVLOS rule (proposed Part 108) has been under White House review since July 2026.

FAA • 2026

Advanced operations continue to expand.

On August 27, 2026, the FAA announced BEYOND Phase 2 would add up to eight new lead participants as it continues work on advanced drone integration, including operations beyond visual line of sight.

BLS • 2025–35

Geospatial careers remain a strong adjacent pathway.

BLS projects 7% employment growth for cartographers and photogrammetrists from 2025 to 2035, with about 900 openings per year; the median annual wage was $81,390 in May 2025. These figures describe that occupation—not every drone-related role.

BLS • 2025–35

Human interpretation still matters as drone tools improve.

Surveyor employment is projected to grow 6% from 2025 to 2035. BLS notes that drones improve efficiency, while professionals still review and interpret outputs for accuracy and completeness.

Training & certification

Certification is one pathway—not the whole career.

Drone Career Explorer offers program-issued certificates of completion for defined learning pathways. These educational credentials are separate from FAA aviation certification. For commercial small-UAS operations under Part 107, the FAA issues the Remote Pilot Certificate. Students may begin learning earlier, but FAA eligibility for a first-time Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate begins at age 16.

Drone Career Explorer recognition

Pathway certificates + educator microcredential

Learners can earn program certificates after completing defined pathway and evidence requirements, while educators can earn a microcredential through professional learning and implementation evidence.

Federal credential

This is the federal credential for operating a small drone under the FAA's Part 107 rules. Schools and training providers may help students prepare, but the certificate itself is issued by the FAA.

For schools & programs

Connect career exploration to training and credentials.

A school does not need to become a certification provider to create a strong pathway. Drone Career Explorer helps students discover the field, build foundational knowledge, connect classroom learning with simulation opportunities where available, and identify postsecondary or FAA-recognized pathways.

1

Explore locally

Use the FAA UAS-CTI directory to identify nearby colleges, universities, and technical programs.

2

Bring industry closer

Invite postsecondary faculty, UAS professionals, or an FAA DronePro to speak, demonstrate careers, or support an awareness event.

3

Connect the pathway

Connect classroom projects to campus visits, simulation experiences, summer programs, dual-enrollment opportunities where available, and certification preparation when appropriate.
